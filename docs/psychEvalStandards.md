# PSYCHIATRIC EVALUATION STANDARDS

## Documentation Guidelines for Consistent Clinical Assessment

---

## CORE PRINCIPLES

### 1. **Patient-Centered Approach**

- **Lead with the patient's voice** - Use their exact words and descriptions whenever possible
- **Prioritize patient strengths** before deficits - Always highlight coping mechanisms, insight, and recovery efforts
- **Avoid pathologizing language** - Frame symptoms as experiences rather than failures
- **Respect patient autonomy** - Document their choices and self-determination efforts

### 2. **Clinical Objectivity with Empathy**

- **Present facts without judgment** - Document substance use as medical history, not moral failing
- **Balance concern with hope** - Acknowledge problems while emphasizing recovery potential
- **Use person-first language** - "Patient with bipolar disorder" not "bipolar patient"
- **Maintain diagnostic humility** - Clearly distinguish between findings, impressions, and confirmed diagnoses

---

## DOCUMENTATION STRUCTURE STANDARDS

### Header Requirements

- **Complete identifying information** including date, duration, setting, and clinician
- **Professional formatting** with clear section breaks and hierarchy
- **Contact information** when relevant for follow-up coordination

### Chief Complaint Section

- **Keep it concise** - One sentence capturing the primary reason for visit
- **Use patient's perspective** - What brought them here today, not what we think they need
- **Avoid clinical jargon** in this section

### History of Present Illness Standards

- **Chronological narrative** that tells the patient's story coherently
- **Quantify when possible** - Specific timeframes, frequencies, durations
- **Include functional context** - How symptoms impact daily life, work, relationships
- **Document coping strategies prominently** - Show what's working, not just what's wrong
- **Balance current state with trajectory** - Where they are vs. where they're heading

---

## CRITICAL DOCUMENTATION PRIORITIES

### 1. **Substance Use Documentation**

- **Complete timeline** - Onset, duration, patterns, recovery attempts
- **Specific substances and quantities** when available
- **Recovery efforts and successes** - Frame as positive clinical progress
- **Current status clearly stated** - No ambiguity about active use
- **Link to neurobiological findings** when relevant, without blame

### 2. **Mental Status Examination**

- **Systematic coverage** of all domains, even if brief
- **Behavioral observations** - What you actually see, not inferences
- **Cognitive assessment** - Include both deficits and strengths
- **Risk assessment integrated** throughout, not isolated
- **Insight and judgment emphasized** - These predict treatment success

### 3. **Historical Context Integration**

- **Previous diagnoses addressed explicitly** - Confirmed, questioned, or refined
- **Treatment history comprehensive** - What worked, what didn't, why
- **Family and social context** when relevant to clinical picture
- **Developmental considerations** - How current issues evolved

---

## DIAGNOSTIC APPROACH STANDARDS

### Diagnostic Formulation Principles

- **Evidence-based reasoning** - Link findings to diagnostic criteria
- **Multiple perspectives considered** - Biological, psychological, social factors
- **Differential diagnosis explicit** - What else could explain these symptoms
- **Uncertainty acknowledged** - Say when you don't know or need more information
- **Avoid premature closure** - Let complexity exist rather than forcing simple answers

### Diagnostic Language Standards

- **"Consistent with" vs. "Definitive diagnosis"** - Be precise about confidence level
- **Separate current state from historical patterns** - Past episodes vs. current functioning
- **Note contributing factors** - Substances, medical conditions, stressors
- **Acknowledge limitations** - What tests weren't done, information unavailable

---

## TREATMENT PLANNING PHILOSOPHY

### 1. **Least Restrictive, Most Effective**

- **Non-pharmacological interventions first** when appropriate and safe
- **Lifestyle modifications as primary treatment** - Exercise, sleep, substance cessation
- **Medication when needed** - Don't withhold when clinically indicated
- **Patient preference incorporated** - Shared decision-making documented

### 2. **Strength-Based Treatment Planning**

- **Build on existing coping skills** - What's already working
- **Support natural recovery processes** - Neuroplasticity, resilience, growth
- **Realistic, achievable goals** - Set patients up for success
- **Specific, measurable interventions** - Clear action steps, not vague recommendations

### 3. **Recovery-Oriented Language**

- **"Recovery potential" not just "prognosis"** - Emphasize growth possibility
- **"Currently stable" vs. "in remission"** - Active wellness rather than absence of illness
- **"Developing skills" vs. "lacking insight"** - Growth mindset framing
- **"Vulnerable periods" vs. "high-risk"** - Realistic without catastrophizing

---

## WRITING STYLE REQUIREMENTS

### Tone and Voice

- **Professional but warm** - Clinical accuracy with human understanding
- **Confident but humble** - Expert knowledge with diagnostic humility
- **Clear and accessible** - Avoid unnecessary jargon, explain technical terms
- **Respectful of patient experience** - Their reality is valid starting point

### Structure and Flow

- **Logical progression** - Each section builds understanding
- **Smooth transitions** between sections - Connect the clinical story
- **Appropriate detail level** - Comprehensive but not overwhelming
- **Scannable format** - Headers, bullets, and white space for readability

### Language Precision

- **Specific over general** - "Daily cannabis use" not "substance abuse"
- **Behavioral over interpretive** - What they did/said, not what it means
- **Temporal specificity** - When things happened, how long they lasted
- **Quantified when possible** - Frequencies, durations, intensities

---

## QUALITY ASSURANCE CHECKLIST

### Before Finalizing Any Evaluation:

- [ ] Patient's strengths and coping skills prominently featured
- [ ] All major clinical domains addressed systematically
- [ ] Substance use history complete and non-judgmental
- [ ] Diagnostic reasoning clearly explained
- [ ] Treatment plan specific and actionable
- [ ] Risk assessment appropriate and contextualized
- [ ] Recovery potential and prognosis realistic but hopeful
- [ ] Follow-up plan clear and appropriate
- [ ] Professional formatting and grammar
- [ ] Patient dignity and autonomy respected throughout

---

## ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS

### Documentation Ethics

- **Accuracy over convenience** - Don't simplify complex presentations
- **Patient benefit over administrative ease** - Serve the patient's needs first
- **Confidentiality maintained** - Appropriate detail level for purpose
- **Cultural sensitivity integrated** - Consider background influences
- **Bias awareness constant** - Check assumptions and stereotypes

### Clinical Stance

- **Therapeutic optimism balanced with realism** - Hope without false promises
- **Patient empowerment prioritized** - Support their agency and choices
- **Harm reduction principles** - Meet patients where they are
- **Recovery-oriented practice** - Believe in possibility of growth and healing

---

## SPECIAL POPULATIONS CONSIDERATIONS

### Young Adults (18-30)

- **Developmental context critical** - Brain maturation, identity formation
- **Substance use normalized as health issue** - Not moral judgment
- **Educational/career impacts considered** - Functional goals appropriate to age
- **Family dynamics acknowledged** - Independence vs. support needs
- **Technology integration realistic** - Gaming, social media as contextual factors

### Dual Diagnosis Patients

- **Integrated treatment approach** - Don't separate mental health from addiction
- **Timeline correlation important** - Which came first, how they interact
- **Recovery stages recognized** - Where they are in both processes
- **Relapse as learning opportunity** - Not treatment failure

---

_These standards ensure consistent, high-quality psychiatric evaluations that honor patient dignity, support recovery, and provide clear clinical guidance for ongoing care. Every evaluation should leave the reader with a clear understanding of who this person is, what they're experiencing, and how we can best support their journey toward wellness._
